# beee-2026-09-10 — Basic Electrical & Electronics Engineering

## Summary

Kirchhoff's two laws, and a worked two-loop circuit using them. The current law
was derived from conservation of charge and the voltage law from conservation
of energy, which the lecturer insisted on doing before either was stated as a
formula — the point being that neither is an independent physical assumption.
Sign conventions took up most of the middle of the class, because they are
where the marks are lost: the rule given was to fix a direction for each loop
first and stay with it, accepting a negative answer as a current that runs the
other way. One example was carried through to two currents of 1 A and 0.5 A.

## Key Points

- **Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)**
  - The sum of currents entering a node equals the sum leaving it
  - $$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$$
  - It is conservation of charge — charge does not pile up at a junction

- **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)**
  - Around any closed loop, the algebraic sum of potential differences is zero
  - $$\sum V = 0$$
  - It is conservation of energy — return to your starting point and you are
    back at the same potential

- **Sign conventions, which is where marks go**
  - Choose a direction to traverse each loop and keep it
  - A rise across a source counts positive, a drop across a resistor negative
    (or consistently the reverse — what matters is consistency)
  - A negative current in the answer means the real current runs opposite to
    the direction you assumed. It is not an error

- **Worked example**
  - Two loops, a 10 V and a 5 V source, resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω
  - KCL at the shared node: $I_3 = I_1 + I_2$
  - KVL round each loop, two equations, two unknowns
  - Result: $I_1 = 1$ A, $I_2 = 0.5$ A, $I_3 = 1.5$ A

## Questions

**1. State Kirchhoff's Current Law and the conservation principle behind it.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The algebraic sum of currents at any node is zero — what flows in equals what
flows out:
$$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$$

It follows from conservation of charge: charge cannot accumulate at a junction,
so it must leave as fast as it arrives.

</details>

**2. State Kirchhoff's Voltage Law and the principle behind it.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Around any closed loop the algebraic sum of potential differences is zero:
$$\sum V = 0$$

It follows from conservation of energy: moving a charge around a loop and back
to its starting point must involve no net change in potential energy.

</details>

**3. Your solution gives a current of $-0.4$ A. What does the minus sign mean?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

That the current has magnitude 0.4 A and flows opposite to the direction you
assumed when setting up the equations. The assumed direction is arbitrary, so a
negative result is the method correcting your guess, not a mistake.

</details>

**4. Two resistors, 3 Ω and 6 Ω, are in parallel across a 12 V source. Find the current drawn from the source.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Parallel combination:
$$\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow R = 2\,\Omega$$

By Ohm's law:
$$I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{12}{2} = 6\ \text{A}$$

Checking with KCL: 4 A through the 3 Ω branch and 2 A through the 6 Ω branch,
totalling 6 A.

</details>
