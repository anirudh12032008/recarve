# evs-2026-09-12 — Environmental Science

## Summary

Ecosystems as energy systems. The class was built around the ten percent law:
roughly a tenth of the energy at one trophic level reaches the next, the rest
lost as heat through respiration. That single number was then used to explain
three things that are usually taught separately — why food chains are short,
why pyramids of energy are always upright even when pyramids of numbers are
not, and why a diet lower down the chain feeds more people from the same land.
Biogeochemical cycles were introduced at the end, with carbon as the worked
example.

## Key Points

- **Trophic levels**
  - Producers → primary consumers → secondary consumers → tertiary consumers
  - Decomposers act on every level rather than sitting at one

- **The ten percent law**
  - About 10% of the energy at one level is available to the next
  - The remaining ~90% is lost, mostly as heat through respiration
  - The figure is an average, not a constant — it ranges widely in practice

- **Three consequences of one number**
  - Food chains rarely exceed 4–5 levels: there is not enough energy left
  - A pyramid of energy is always upright, because energy only decreases
  - A pyramid of numbers can invert — one tree supports many insects — but the
    energy pyramid underneath it cannot

- **Carbon cycle, in outline**
  - Photosynthesis fixes atmospheric CO₂ into organic matter
  - Respiration and decomposition return it
  - Combustion of fossil fuels returns carbon that had been out of circulation
    for millions of years, which is the imbalance that matters

## Questions

**1. State the ten percent law and name where the missing energy goes.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next.
The other ~90% is lost — largely as heat through respiration, with the rest
unassimilated or never consumed.

</details>

**2. Why are food chains usually limited to four or five trophic levels?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Because of the compounding loss. Starting from 10,000 units at the producers,
a fifth level receives roughly 1 unit. That is too little to support a viable
population, so the chain ends.

</details>

**3. A pyramid of numbers can be inverted but a pyramid of energy cannot. Explain.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A pyramid of numbers counts organisms, and one large producer — a single tree —
can support thousands of small consumers, which inverts the shape.

A pyramid of energy measures energy flow, and energy is always lost moving up a
level. Each level therefore holds strictly less than the one below it, so the
pyramid is always upright.

</details>

**4. Why does a vegetarian diet support more people from the same area of land?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Eating plants places you at the second trophic level; eating animals that ate
plants places you at the third. One extra level costs roughly 90% of the
available energy, so the same land supports about ten times as many people when
the intermediate level is removed.

</details>
